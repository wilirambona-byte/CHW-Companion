CHW Companion

AI-Powered Clinical Decision Support for Community Health Workers

CHW Companion is a WhatsApp-based AI tool designed to support — not replace — community health workers during home visits and patient encounters in low-resource settings. Voice in, text out, grounded in national clinical protocols.

The Problem

A Health Workforce Crisis, Concentrated in Africa

Community health workers are the backbone of primary healthcare in sub-Saharan Africa — often the first and only point of contact between patients and the health system. Yet the world faces a shortage of 15 million health workers, and Africa bears the brunt: the WHO African region's shortage is projected to constitute 52% of the global gap by 2030.

CHWs carry the weight of this reality with limited training, scarce diagnostic tools, and no easy access to clinical guidance in the field. They need real-time support at the point of care — in their language, through a channel they already use, without adding to their workload.

Evidence Base

Can AI Actually Help Frontline Health Workers?

The question is not whether LLMs perform well on medical exams designed for doctors. It is whether they can help frontline health workers, in non-English languages, on the questions those workers actually face. The most relevant evidence says yes — with caveats we take seriously.

4.49/5

LLM Clinical Quality Score

Best-performing models scored 4.49 out of 5 across 11 expert-rated clinical quality dimensions, significantly outperforming local clinicians (P < 0.001), on 5,609 real questions from 101 Rwandan CHWs (Rutunda et al., 2026).

>500×

Cheaper Per Response

LLM responses cost an average of US$0.0035, compared with US$5.43 from a GP. Even in Kinyarwanda, performance remained superior to clinicians at US$0.0044 per response (Rutunda et al., 2026).

3 Trials

Real-World Evidence Underway

Three LLM clinical decision support trials are running across Kenya, Nigeria, and Rwanda — including the first RCT of an LLM on real patient outcomes in Africa, enrolling 9,000 patients (Mateen et al., 2025).

Honest

We Know What We Don't Know

Benchmarking tested single Q&A exchanges, not real clinical encounters. The measured LLM advantage partly reflects evaluator preference for thoroughness. Field trials must confirm whether patient outcomes actually improve. CHW Companion is designed to be the implementation step that the evidence calls for.

The Solution

What the CHW Companion Is — and What It Is Not

A CHW sends a voice note describing a patient's symptoms. The Companion replies with concise, evidence-based guidance drawn from WHO protocols, Africa CDC guidance, and the national CHW service package. The CHW reads the response, uses their own judgement, and acts.

The Companion IS

  • ✔ A clinical decision support tool for CHWs
  • ✔ Designed around voice input and text output
  • ✔ Built for low-connectivity environments with SMS fallback
  • ✔ A tool that supports referral pathways
  • ✔ Invoked at the CHW's discretion, within their existing workflow
  • ✔ An adaptable platform with country-specific deployments

The Companion IS NOT

  • ✘ A replacement for the CHW's own judgement
  • ✘ A diagnostic system or medical device (SaMD)
  • ✘ A substitute for a doctor, nurse, or clinical supervision
  • ✘ A telemedicine platform for patients
  • ✘ A parallel system that adds reporting burden
  • ✘ A standalone solution without human oversight

Process

How It Works

Voice in, text out. No new apps, no typing, no new behaviour — just the WhatsApp voice notes CHWs already use every day, channelled into clinical support.

01
🎙️

CHW Sends a Voice Note

The health worker describes the patient's symptoms via WhatsApp voice note in their local language — as naturally as speaking to a colleague.

02
🧠

AI Analyses and Responds

The voice note is transcribed and processed by an LLM grounded in WHO, Africa CDC, and national CHW protocols via retrieval-augmented generation. A text response is returned on WhatsApp.

03
📋

CHW Acts, Visit is Documented

The CHW reads the guidance, applies their own judgement, and acts. The conversation itself serves as the visit record — no additional documentation step required.

04
👁️

Supervisors See More

A rules-based layer monitors for red-flag escalations, pattern anomalies, and generates weekly digests. Supervisors see more without the CHW doing more.

Clinical Governance

How It Stays Grounded

A tool that gives clinical advice to health workers in the field carries real risk. We take this seriously. The following safeguards are built into the design from day one.

📚

Retrieval Over Verified Protocols

The LLM does not rely on training data alone. A RAG architecture retrieves guidance from WHO IMCI/IMAI, Africa CDC, and the national CHW protocol, chunked by clinical decision point. National protocol takes precedence where sources disagree.

🩺

CHW Remains the Decision-Maker

Responses are framed as suggestions, not instructions. The CHW's clinical judgement, local knowledge, and direct observation of the patient are irreplaceable. This is a design principle, not a legal disclaimer.

🚨

Red-Flag Escalation

When the AI recommends an urgent referral — severe pneumonia, eclampsia, danger signs in a newborn — the supervisor and receiving facility are alerted immediately via deterministic rules-based triggers.

🌍

Language-Stratified Safety Monitoring

LLM performance in low-resource languages is measurably lower than in English. Clinical quality metrics are monitored by language of interaction, so safety-relevant performance drops can be detected and addressed.

Sustainable Design

A Tool That Teaches CHWs to Think

Most AI clinical support tools think for the health worker. The CHW Companion is designed to teach the health worker to think. The goal is not permanent dependency on the tool, but progressive clinical competence that outlasts it.

⚠️

The Deskilling Risk Is Real

When AI does the clinical reasoning for health workers, there is a risk that their own skills erode over time. A 2025 multicentre study found that doctors who regularly used AI during colonoscopies showed a significant drop in detection performance when the AI was removed (Budzyń et al., 2025). The mechanism — cognitive offloading to technology — applies wherever AI replaces rather than reinforces human judgment.

🧩

Guided Reasoning, Not Passive Reading

Instead of always displaying the classification immediately, the Companion can prompt the CHW to reason first: "You measured a mid-upper arm circumference of 11.2 cm for a child aged 18 months. What does that indicate?" The CHW arrives at the answer through guided reasoning. Correct answers are confirmed and reinforced. Incorrect answers are walked back through the clinical logic — in this case, that a MUAC below 11.5 cm signals severe acute malnutrition requiring urgent referral. This is skill-building by design.

📈

Progressive Autonomy

The tool tracks each CHW's assessment accuracy over time. As a CHW demonstrates consistent correct reasoning for a given condition, AI-assisted prompts are progressively reduced. The data the tool generates becomes the evidence for when a CHW no longer needs it. What remains is not a tool-dependent worker, but a skilled one.

💡

A Different Investment Logic

Most AI tools assume the technology must run indefinitely. This design inverts that assumption. Once CHWs reach reliable clinical competence, programmes can reduce API costs, connectivity demands, and device dependency. This is not just a learning feature. It is a sustainability strategy for health systems where electricity, connectivity, and financing remain structural constraints.

Inclusive Design

Gender Sensitivity

Most CHWs in sub-Saharan Africa are women. In rural communities shaped by patriarchal norms, women may face barriers to digital technology use. Three years of programme implementation in Burundi has surfaced consistent feedback on these challenges — they are documented observations, not hypotheses.

🎙️

Voice Input Removes the Literacy Barrier

CHWs who are less comfortable typing — disproportionately women in contexts where girls' education was deprioritised — interact using the voice notes they already send every day.

📱

No Visible "AI App" on the Phone

The tool works within WhatsApp. There is no separate application that might attract attention or require explanation in households where women's phone use is scrutinised.

🔊

Gender-Matched Voice (Planned)

If text-to-speech is introduced in future versions, the system will offer gender-matched voice options. This feature will be tested during the pilot, not assumed effective.

💪

Professional Empowerment

Research from Mozambique found that female CHWs reported increased status when equipped with mHealth tools (Kinshella et al., 2022). CHW Companion is designed with this potential in mind.

Governance

Privacy by Design

Patient data is sensitive. The CHW Companion is built with privacy protection at its core, aligned with WHO's 2021 guidance on ethics and governance of AI for health.

🔒

No Identifiable Patient Data

CHWs describe symptoms without names or ID numbers. No identifiable data is stored by the AI system.

🤝

Community-Level Consent

Consent is sought from local leaders and community structures, recognising that community consent carries significant weight alongside individual consent.

🏛️

Data Sovereignty

Health trends and usage patterns belong to the community and national health system. Data is stored in-country or in a jurisdiction agreed with the national health authority.

🔐

End-to-End Encryption

Conversations are encrypted in transit via WhatsApp. No content is used for model training without explicit authorisation.

Phased Approach

Roadmap

The tool evolves in phases. Each version has a clear scope, a clear evaluation question, and an explicit dependency on the previous version. We do not promise everything at once.

Version 1
Core Clinical Support — Burundi Pilot

WhatsApp voice-in/text-out. Gemini 2.5 Flash + RAG over WHO, Africa CDC, and Burundian CHW protocols. Kirundi speech-to-text via Kinyarwanda proxy. Red-flag alerts. Pilot with a defined CHW cohort.

Version 2
Supervisor Integration & System Connectivity

Supervisor dashboard with weekly digests and QA sampling. FHIR-compliant data export. Initial DHIS2/CHT integration. Language-stratified safety monitoring.

Version 3
Proactive Support & Platform Generalisation

Morning briefings combining patient roster, follow-ups, and climate-informed clinical priming. Multi-language platform generalisation enabling new country deployments in 4–8 weeks. Gender-sensitive feature evaluation.
